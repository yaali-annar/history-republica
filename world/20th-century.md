# The 20th Century

## The Long Peace

The name came later. In 1905, nobody called it that. The Final War had just ended, the Covenant was dissolving, and the immediate work was reconstruction: reconstituting broken states, negotiating the post-Covenant settlement, absorbing Alaska into the Northwest Territory, watching the UKSA fragment into its constituent republics one by one. The name the century eventually gave to itself was not available at its beginning.

By 2000, it is simply what the century is called. The Long Peace. One hundred years without a major war between recognised states. The League of Nations, which the cynics of 1905 expected to follow the Maritime League into ceremonial irrelevance, turned out to be the instrument that made it hold.

## Africa After the Covenant

Covenant Africa did not survive the Covenant. Its whole structure had depended on Iberian shipping and on money from Covenant capitals, and both stopped in 1905.

Prussia's colonies fell first. Kamerun and Prussian East Africa had never been supplied except through Iberian ports, and with the Iberian navy withdrawn to home waters their garrisons could not be reinforced, paid, or evacuated in good order. The League placed both under **transitional commissions**, the first instruments of their kind, charged with administering the territories until governments of their own peoples could take them over. Most of the Kamerun commission's administrators came from Liberia, and in particular from the Krio civil service of Freetown. The East African commission worked in partnership with the Sultanate of Zanzibar and drew heavily on the Cape Republic.

The Pink Strip passed through two decades of negotiation into the South African Federation (see `factions/south-african-federation.md`). Iberian Bissau lost its garrison within a year and its governor within two. Russia's Tadjoura station was given up in the Russian retreat from every commitment it could not pay for, and Ethiopia, which had never been anyone's colony, found itself without a patron for the first time in a generation and turned to the League.

The League's two African republics came out of the Covenant era with a standing they had not had before it. Liberia and the Cape Republic had held the League's positions in Africa through the scramble, and in its aftermath they became the League's principal intermediaries on the continent: Liberia in the west, the Cape in the south. The **Constitution of 1931**, which admitted Liberia's interior as six Hinterland Provinces and created its Council of Chiefs, was studied closely by the transitional commissions as they wrote the constitutions of the states that succeeded them.
