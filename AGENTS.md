# history-republica

## Project Overview

In-world documents from an alternate history in which England and the Dutch Republic never became monarchies and remain republics into the 21st century. Every document is written from the **Straits Federal District** in the year **2000**, as if produced by institutions, publishers, or individuals of that world.

## Key Historical Divergences

### England
- **Oliver Cromwell** is mortally wounded at Worcester (3 September 1651) and dies within weeks, before the Navigation Act (9 October 1651) and before he can consolidate personal rule.
- **John Lambert** (1619-1691) takes over and governs through his **Instrument of Government** as Lord Protector. He resigns voluntarily in 1674, five years before the constitutional age limit of 60, to test the succession system, and remains an elder statesman until his death.

### Dutch Republic
- **Johan de Witt** (1625-c.1697) survives the 1672 mob attack. There is no Orangist consolidation.

### France
- France's failed reconquest of **Nouvelle Gaule** (republican from 1776) bankrupts the crown.
- **Louis XVI** secretly offers **Saint-Domingue to Spain** for military help against the republicans. The offer becomes public and destroys his support.
- The monarchy falls in **1789**. **Louis Capet** flees to Spain, which neither shelters nor expels him. Royalism stays marginal.
- The **French Republic** joins the Maritime League and recognises all prior colonial independence declarations.

## World Structure

> **Orientation note** (for authors, never for in-world text): the **United Nations of Great Britain and Ireland** ("the UN") is this world's counterpart of the United Kingdom, but it is a union of three sovereign republics sharing only foreign policy, defence, and currency. "UN" always means this body. The global body is the **League of Nations**.

- **The Maritime League** (later League of Nations): founded by the **Sister Republics**, England and the Dutch Republic. Republics only. Members include the Union of States, Nouvelle Gaule, the Republic of Meuse, France, and others. States it supports but will not admit hold **associate** status: they observe the Council without seat or vote (the Northern Realm, the Ottoman Empire, Persia, and Ayiti before 1907). The League of Nations replaces it with broader membership. See `misc/maritime-league-institutions.md`.
- **The Covenant**: Iberia, Austria, Prussia, and Russia. Collapses in 1905 after Russia's defeat by Japan. See `factions/covenant.md`.
- **Nusantara Federation** (modern Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines): federation of sultanates plus the Lanfang Republic, never colonised. The Sister Republics held only leased trading posts. Its capital, the **Straits Federal District**, was formed from two leases: Tumasik (English) and Batam (Dutch).
- **Ungoverned territories**: states govern only as far as control is worth the cost. The **Sahara** interior (Tuareg and others) and the **Amazon basin** interior are under no effective sovereignty. The League holds that their peoples must not be colonised but enforces this only diplomatically.

## Key Canon Dates

### The British Isles
- **The UN**: England and Scotland, mid-17th century, for foreign policy, defence, and currency. England holds the permanent chair. English members sometimes say "Britain", the Irish do not.
- **Scotland**: no permanent executive. Parliamentary committee government with a rotating Speaker. Sends a **Commissioner to the UN**.
- **Ireland** joins in **1700**. Its Catholic gentry kept their property under Lambert's settlement.
- **Act of Union, 1707**: renamed the **United Nations of Great Britain and Ireland**. Secretariat to **Berwick**. Unanimity for major decisions, so each nation has a veto.
- **Lord Protectors**: Lambert (to 1674), then **Algernon Sidney** (1674-1683). The office then becomes the **Chairman of Parliament**, elected for a fixed term.
- **Catholics** could vote but not hold office under the Commonwealth. The 1707 Act lifts the bar at UN level. Only Ireland returns Catholic MPs for generations.

### The Dutch Republic
- **Flanders and Brabant** join as provinces after the War of Devolution (1668), making nine. See `factions/dutch-republic.md`.

### The Americas
- **Treaty Day, 1763**: the **Union of States** is constituted by negotiation with England and the Dutch Republic after the Seven Years War and joins the League. Its founding document abolishes hereditary slavery, a League condition (Dutch half-freedom tradition, Quaker influence, no entrenched slave codes). See `factions/union-of-states.md`.
- **Nouvelle Gaule**: all of French America, republican from July 1776. After Canadia's secession it is Louisiana and the Ohio country, centred on New Orleans.
- **Canadia**: the Catholic, francophone St. Lawrence settlements, which secede in the early 19th century. The trigger is the Republican Calendar breaking the seven-day week needed for Sunday observance. Joins the League after secession.
- **Saint-Domingue** declares independence in 1789, abolishes slavery, and adopts the Republican Calendar. Its occupation of Spanish **Santo Domingo** and the killing of the Spanish governor feed Iberian unification. It federates with Santo Domingo as the **Federal Republic of Ayiti** (1823). The killing keeps it a League associate until 1907. See `factions/ayiti.md`.
- **Gran Colombia**: Venezuela, New Granada, and Ecuador, from Bolívar's campaigns (1820s). Covenant-backed Iberian reconquest fails. Iberia keeps Peru and the south. See `factions/gran-colombia.md`.
- **UKSA** (c. 1840-1905): Iberia's remaining South American territories under the Bragança crown. Fragments after 1905 into Brazil, Argentina, Peru, Chile, Bolivia (Aymara/Quechua majority), Uruguay, Paraguay (Guaraní character), and an independent **Mapuche** League member. See `factions/uksa.md`.
- **Kingdom of the Malvinas**: Bragança remnant, the only non-League state in the western hemisphere. Banking haven known for the **Soledad Box**. Argentina claims it but does not press the claim. See `factions/malvinas.md`.
- **Guyanas Federation**: English, Dutch, and French Guiana, federated c. 1795 under Iberian pressure. See `factions/guyanas-federation.md`.

### Continental Europe
- **The Covenant** forms 1800-1815 (not 1789), triggered by the **Republic of Rhine** declaring inside HRE territory in the late 1790s. The Rhine borders Meuse and the Dutch Republic, is surrounded by the Covenant on three sides, and survives under implicit League guarantee.
- **HRE**: Austria and Prussia absorb the smaller members. Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck and other coastal cities negotiate neutrality.
- **Bernadotte** commands on the Rhine. His treatment of Prussian prisoners leads to an offer from the **Northern Realm**, where he becomes **High Guardian** rather than king. The Realm later exports this constitutional model to Ottoman, Persian, and Japanese reformers. See `factions/northern-realm.md`.
- **Napoleon** commands in northern Italy against Austria and builds the **Genoa-Venice** road, later rail. The settlement leaves League-aligned republics on Austria's southern border.
- **Iberian Empire** (~1790): the 1785 marriage of **João of Portugal** and **Carlota Joaquina of Spain** turned into political union by the Santo Domingo Affair. The Covenant's only maritime and colonial power. Holds Gibraltar. See `factions/iberian-empire.md`.
- **Colonial Protocol** (1820s): Prussia and Austria supply settlers, soldiers, and capital to Iberian colonies for a share of revenue, and Russia supplies gold. The **Protocol Revision** (1874) pays Iberia's arrears to Prussia in African sovereignty: **Kamerun** (1876) and the **East African interior** (1880s). Austria declines colonies.
- **Russia** takes the Bosphorus from the Ottomans in the mid-19th century. The **Ottoman Empire** loses the Balkans but survives in Anatolia and the Arab lands with League support.

### Africa
- **League presence**: two republics, the **Cape Republic** (1784) and **Liberia** (1847). Elsewhere treaties and leased ports, not conquest. Partners include Asante, the Yoruba states, Sokoto, Zanzibar, Ottoman Egypt, and the southern kingdoms brought in through the Cape.
- **Convention of The Hague** (1792): League members end the slave trade by their own ships and treat it as piracy in League waters. Iberia continues the trade to Brazil and Cuba until the 1860s.
- **Liberia**: covers both Sierra Leone and Liberia. A joint English and Union venture: Sharp's **Province of Freedom** at Freetown (1787) becomes the **Sierra Leone Company** (1791), chartered by both, with boards in London and Philadelphia. Union emigrants arrive from 1792, and the joint West Africa Squadron (1794) lands **Recaptives**, who become the **Krio**. The company's second settlement, **Christopolis**, is founded 1804-1808 (the African Settlement Society, Paul Cuffe). The **Gallinas Question** forces statehood in 1847: the Squadron may only blockade the Gallinas slave coast, Iberia claims it by treaty and from 1842 escorts slavers with warships, and a chartered company cannot answer a sovereign, so the sponsors constitute the company's settlements as a republic. Iberia arms interior chiefs from Bissau from the 1860s. The **Hinterland War** (1898-1899) is started by Iberian-aligned northern chiefdoms and won by Liberia's Mende and Temne allies, who then accept Liberian jurisdiction in the **Bo Accord** (1899). The Constitution of 1931 adds six Hinterland Provinces and a **Council of Chiefs**. Stable in 2000. See `factions/liberia.md`.
- **Covenant Africa**: Iberia holds the Pink Strip, Congo basin claims, Bissau and Cape Verde, the Gulf of Guinea islands and Río Muni, and northern Morocco (from 1860). Prussia holds Kamerun and East Africa. Russia aligns with **Ethiopia** and holds Tadjoura (1889). The **Lisbon Conference** (1884) sets the effective-administration doctrine. After 1905 Prussian Africa passes to League transitional commissions.

### East Asia
- **Japan** (*Nihon Shōhan*, Japanese Domains): League-aligned confederation of domains. After the Lords' War (1870s) the Domain faction adopts a constitution on the Northern Realm model. The **Tennō** is religious head only, in Kyoto. The **Shogun** is elected by the Council of Lords for a fixed term and governs from Edo. Japan's defeat of Russia in 1905 brings down the Covenant. See `factions/japanese-domains.md`.
- **Ryukyu**: protected by Satsuma from Qing pressure, then an independent kingdom and League member from 1905.
- **Korea**: Qing protectorate. The Joseon court suppressed **Canadien Catholic missionaries** as a threat to royal authority, and its Covenant alignment ran through the Qing. Post-1905 path unsettled.
- **Qing**: loses southern China in a Taiping-like crisis (1850s-1860s) and keeps Manchuria, Mongolia, Tibet, Xinjiang, and the north. Reconstitutes after 1905 as the **Middle Empire** (*Dulimbai Gurun*). Constitutional form TBD. See `factions/qing-successor.md`.
- **Southern China**: League-aligned Han republic, makes no claim on the Qing's Inner Asian lands, called "China" internationally by 2000. Internal name and structure TBD.

## The Documents

The project produces in-universe texts (entries, articles, circulars, and so on) that could plausibly exist in this 2000.

**All documents are strictly in-world.** Their writers know no other world. Never refer to "other timelines", "our world", or counterfactuals, and never frame events as avoiding some other outcome. Things happened as they happened.

## Development Notes

- Worldbuilding happens in feature branches. `main` holds settled canon.
- **Search the repository before asking about any person, faction, event, or concept.** Existing canon may already answer it.

### Canon Directories
- `world/`: historical overviews and period documents
- `people/`: biographical dossiers
- `factions/`: states and organisations
- `misc/`: topics that belong to no single faction or period (calendars, institutions, conventions)

### Document Conventions
- Before writing a new faction document, read `factions/template.md`. Do not copy the structure of existing faction files instead.
- **No editorialising.** State events, causes, and institutions plainly and let the reader draw conclusions. Cut narrator verdicts ("worked better than its critics expected"), historians or commentators used to voice the author's opinion, closing ironies and quips, and moralising comparisons that add no fact. A reason a party acted is fine when stated as a plain cause.
- **No em-dashes and no semicolons, anywhere, in any document.** Use a period, comma, colon, or parenthetical instead. This applies to every file in the repository.
